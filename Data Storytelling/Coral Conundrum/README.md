# **🐠 The Coral Conundrum: Solving the 1998 Frequency Mystery**

### **📜 The Narrative**

In **1998**, three major global events collided: the UN declared it the **"International Year of the Ocean,"** a massive global **coral bleaching event** occurred due to El Niño, and **Google** was officially launched, marking the dawn of the modern internet. Paradoxically, instead of a surge in interest, the written frequency of almost all coral reef fish species (like the *Clownfish* and *Butterflyfish*) saw a **sharp, dramatic decline.** This project is a digital detective story aimed at finding out why.

---

### **🧠 The Hypothesis**

We investigate three competing "storytelling" theories for this decline:

1. **The Conservation Crisis:** Did the bleaching event make these species a "depressing" topic, leading to less coverage?
2. **The Digital Shift:** Did the rise of the internet change how we catalog and name species, moving away from formal printed mentions?
3. **The Awareness Paradox:** Did broad terms like "Year of the Ocean" overshadow specific species' names in global discourse?

---

### **🛠 Data Craftsmanship**

To solve the mystery, I employed:

* **Anomaly Detection:** Identifying the exact "inflection point" where mentions of diverse species (Triggerfish, Parrotfish, Angelfish) synchronized in a downward trend.
* **Temporal Correlation:** Mapping external global milestones (UN designations, tech launches) against species-specific frequency data.
* **Digital Access Modeling:** Analyzing the impact of internet consolidation on how information about niche biological species is consumed and recorded.

Deep Dive Analysis
The 1998 Inflection Point: Almost all analyzed fish species (e.g., Clownfish, Parrotfish) show a peak in the mid-1990s followed by a steep, unexplained drop in 1998.

Internet vs. Reality: While 1998 was the "International Year of the Ocean," the decline is too consistent across unrelated species to be purely environmental.

Digital Outliers: The analysis explores if "Digital Access" changed the data. As the internet grew, the way books were digitized or the way "frequency" was calculated in global corpora (like Google Ngrams) shifted, creating a statistical "outlier" period where traditional word frequency appears to crash.

---

### **📈 Discovery & Insights**

* **The Universal Drop:** The decline wasn't limited to one species; it was a systemic shift across the entire reef fish category, suggesting a change in **media/information structure** rather than biological extinction.
* **The Internet Factor:** As the world moved from books/journals to social media and blogs, the "Ease of Digital Access" changed. Information became consolidated; niche names often got lost in the vastness of broader digital topics.
* **Campaign vs. Reality:** While the "Year of the Ocean" raised awareness for the *environment*, it didn't necessarily translate to more mentions of specific fish, highlighting a gap between high-level conservation and detailed biological storytelling.
