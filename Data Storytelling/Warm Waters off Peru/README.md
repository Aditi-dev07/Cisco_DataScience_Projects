## 🌊 **Warm Waters off Peru**
### **Detecting El Niño’s Impact on Marine Life Through Language and Climate Data**

---

### **🔍 Project Overview**
El Niño is a recurring climate phenomenon characterized by unusually warm ocean waters in the eastern Pacific, particularly off the coast of Peru. These warming events dramatically disrupt marine ecosystems, fisheries, and weather patterns across the globe.

This project investigates a key question:

> **Can large-scale climate events like El Niño be detected indirectly through changes in language related to marine life?**

By combining **sea surface temperature data** with **historical word-frequency trends**, this project explores how environmental disruption influences both ecosystems and public discourse.

---

### **🎯 Project Objectives**
- Identify El Niño events using ocean temperature anomalies  
- Analyze word-frequency trends for marine species affected by warm waters  
- Examine whether climatic disruptions correspond with linguistic changes  

---

### **🗂️ Data Description**
#### **Primary Datasets**
- **Sea Surface Temperature (SST) Data**
  - Region: Eastern Pacific (off the coast of Peru)
  - Used to identify periods of anomalously warm water
- **Historical English Word-Frequency Corpus**
  - Time-series frequency data for marine-related terms

#### **Focus Terms**
- Marine species sensitive to temperature changes
- Ocean- and fisheries-related keywords

---

### **🔧 Data Preprocessing**
- Identified El Niño years using SST anomaly thresholds  
- Normalized word-frequency data for cross-period comparison  
- Aligned climate data timelines with linguistic trends  

---

### **🔬 Methodology**
This project applies **event-based exploratory analysis**, using El Niño events as natural experiments.

#### **Analytical Approach**
- Time-series visualization of SST anomalies  
- Overlay of El Niño periods on word-frequency trends  
- Comparative analysis of language usage during normal vs warm-water years  

#### **Rationale**
Climate events affect ecosystems first, but their impacts often ripple outward—eventually influencing economics, culture, and language.

---

### **📈 Visualizations & Narrative Evidence**
The analysis includes:
- Line plots of sea surface temperature anomalies  
- Highlighted El Niño periods  
- Word-frequency trends for affected marine species  
---

### **🧭 Interpretation Focus**

Periods of unusually warm water off the coast of Peru align with noticeable changes in word usage related to marine life. These patterns suggest that large-scale climate disruptions influence not only ecosystems, but also how frequently affected species enter public discourse.

---

### **✅ Results & Key Insights**

* El Niño events correspond with **distinct shifts in marine-related language usage**
* Certain species-related terms decline or change trajectory during warm-water periods
* Linguistic patterns reflect ecological stress and changing human interaction with marine environments

**Key Insight:**
Climate events leave measurable traces not just in temperature records—but in the language people use to describe the natural world.
