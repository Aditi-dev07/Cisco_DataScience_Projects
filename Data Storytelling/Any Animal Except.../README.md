## 🐅 **Any Animal Except…**
### **How British Menageries Quietly Changed the English Language (1750–1835)**

---

### **🔍 Project Overview**
During the late 18th and early 19th centuries, Britain experienced a surge in public fascination with exotic animals through the rise of **menageries**—early zoological exhibitions that introduced animals such as tigers, toucans, and ostriches to the public.

This project explores a compelling question:

> **Does cultural exposure to new phenomena leave measurable traces in language?**

By analyzing historical word-frequency data, the project investigates whether the introduction of exotic animals into British society corresponded with increased usage of their names in English texts.

---

### **🎯 Project Objectives**
- Examine language usage trends for exotic animal names over time  
- Identify whether word frequencies shift during the British menagerie era  
- Demonstrate how historical and cultural events can be analyzed through data  

---

### **🗂️ Data Description**
#### **Data Source**
- Historical English word-frequency corpus  
- Time span covering **pre-, during, and post-menagerie era**

#### **Key Features**
- Exotic animal keywords including:
  - `tiger`, `hyena`, `tapir`, `toucan`, `macaw`, `ostrich`
- Time-series frequency values (yearly resolution)

#### **Data Preprocessing**
- Filtered corpus to retain only animal-related terms
- Normalized word frequencies to enable fair comparison across time
- Annotated the **menagerie period (1750–1835)** for contextual analysis

---

### **🔬 Methodology**
This project emphasizes **interpretability and storytelling** over complex modeling.

#### **Analytical Techniques**
- Exploratory time-series analysis
- Comparative trend analysis across multiple animal terms
- Custom reusable visualization function (`plot_animal()`)
---
#### **Rationale**
Time-series visualization is particularly effective for identifying cultural or historical shifts that align with known societal events.
---
### **📈 Visualizations & Graphical Analysis**
Each visualization includes:
- A **line plot** showing word-frequency trends over time  
- A shaded region highlighting the **British menagerie era**  
- Post-era trends for historical comparison  
---
